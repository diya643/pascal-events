InvestOps Asia 2026: Six Lessons on AI for Investment Operations

We attended InvestOps Asia 2026 in Singapore to understand how investment operations leaders at asset managers and asset owners are approaching AI, data and their operating models.

Dates
August 19 to 20, 2026
Venue
One Farrer Hotel, Singapore
Format
Conference
Organizer

About the Event

InvestOps Asia brings together more than 200 heads of investment operations and regional COOs from across the buy side. Operations is where AI has to prove itself under the strictest controls, so we went to hear what is working, what is stuck and what these teams need from technology providers.

The timing was useful. On the first morning, the Monetary Authority of Singapore announced new measures to attract more fund managers to the city. Operations leaders were already thinking about what that growth would demand from their teams and systems.

Six Lessons for Operations Teams

  1. One Version of the Truth Comes First

    Operations leaders agreed that AI cannot be trusted on top of fragmented data. Most of the successful AI projects we heard about started as data consolidation projects that created a single reliable record before adding automation.

  2. Moving From Pilot to Production Is the Hard Part

    Getting a proof of concept to work is no longer the challenge. The blockers come later: handling exceptions, getting sign-off from control functions and connecting to older systems that were never designed to share data.

  3. AI Should Extend Operations Teams

    Operations leaders described AI as a way to give their teams more capacity. Reconciliations, investigating breaks and processing documents were the most common use cases, with people keeping the final say.

  4. Private Markets Bring More Documents

    As firms add private market assets, operations teams handle more capital call notices, valuation reports and side letters, almost all of them as documents. Pulling reliable data out of those documents was the capability we were asked about most.

  5. Teams Want Fewer Vendors

    Many operations teams run dozens of separate tools. They want fewer providers that each do more, and they now ask any new vendor which existing system it will replace.

  6. Regulation Is Becoming More Specific

    Firms expect regulators to hold them accountable for any AI they use, including AI bought from outside providers. Singapore's regulator has since issued AI risk management guidelines that do exactly that, and that explicitly cover AI agents.

Our Take

We work with operations teams on exactly these problems. The Pascal Data Agent organizes documents and extracts the data inside them, and our work with asset servicers shows how this runs inside a firm's own environment with its permissions carried through.
