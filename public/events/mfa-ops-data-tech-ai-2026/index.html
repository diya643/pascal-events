MFA Ops: Data, Tech & AI 2026: What Hedge Fund Technology Leaders Are Prioritizing

We attended MFA Ops: Data, Tech & AI 2026 in New York to hear how technology leaders at hedge funds and alternative managers are planning their next year of AI, data and infrastructure work.

Date
November 10, 2026
Venue
11 Madison Avenue, New York
Format
Conference
Organizer
Managed Funds Association

About the Event

The Managed Funds Association's one-day forum, hosted by UBS on November 10, brings together the people who run technology, data and operations at alternative investment firms. Its 2026 program centered on using AI responsibly, turning data into an advantage and building the operational infrastructure funds will need next.

For us it was a chance to test what we hear in client conversations against a room of CTOs, CIOs and heads of data. The timing mattered too: the SEC had just published its 2027 examination priorities, which include how firms use advanced AI in their operations and how they supervise it.

Six Priorities We Heard

  1. Data Work Comes Before AI Results

    The firms getting real value from AI did the data work first: connecting internal systems, agreeing on definitions and deciding how AI-generated data will itself be governed. Standards such as the Model Context Protocol (MCP) are becoming the common way AI tools connect to that data.

  2. Firms Are Letting Non-Engineers Build Tools

    Many firms now run internal AI labs where analysts and operations staff build their own tools. The open question is governance: who reviews what gets built, who maintains it and when it gets retired.

  3. Measuring AI Returns Is Still Hard

    Usage numbers say little about value. Firms are starting to measure hours saved and results improved on specific workflows, while keeping an eye on rising model and computing costs.

  4. AI Agents Need Identities and Limits

    An agent acting for a firm needs its own identity, with permissions, limits on what it can do and a full record of its actions, much like a new employee. Firms are working out how much autonomy to give agents and how to test them safely first.

  5. Private Credit Is Moving Off Spreadsheets

    Private credit teams are replacing Excel-based processes with AI-native systems. Their work is document-heavy, which makes it one of the fastest areas for extraction and monitoring agents to pay off.

  6. Firms Want Fewer, Deeper Platforms

    Vendor consolidation came up throughout the day. Firms are deciding what to keep, what to replace and what to build themselves, and they want platforms that work with the systems and data they already have.

Our Take

These priorities match how we deploy Pascal at hedge funds: autonomous agents with permissions the firm sets, a context graph built on the firm's own data, connections through MCP and APIs, and controls designed to stand up to an examination, as described on our security page.
