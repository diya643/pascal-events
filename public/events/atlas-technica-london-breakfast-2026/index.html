Where Alpha Sits When Every Fund Has the Same Models: A London Breakfast With Atlas Technica

We co-hosted a private breakfast at The Connaught in London with Atlas Technica to ask alternative investment leaders one question: if every fund can buy the same AI models, where does the edge come from?

Date
June 2026
Venue
The Connaught, Mayfair, London
Format
Invite-only breakfast
Hosts
Pascal AI Labs and Atlas Technica

About the Event

Atlas Technica provides technology and AI advisory services to alternative investment firms, and many of its clients are working through the same AI decisions we see in our own deployments. In June, we brought together operations, technology, data and investment leaders from hedge funds and alternative managers at The Connaught in Mayfair.

Vibhav Viswanathan, our CEO and Co-Founder, and Alex Vayner, Global Head of AI Advisory and Solutions at Atlas Technica, opened with what they see in live deployments. The rest of the morning was an open discussion around the tables.

What the Room Agreed On

  1. The Edge Comes From Infrastructure

    Every fund in the room had access to the same frontier models, so the model itself gives no one an advantage. The difference comes from the layer around it: how a fund protects its proprietary data and how precisely it supplies the right context for each task. Two funds can run the same model on the same filing and get very different value, depending on what their system knows about their own portfolio.

  2. Every Answer Must Be Verifiable

    Funds using AI agents will not act on an output they cannot check. Every answer needs a clear trail back to its source and has to be defensible to a portfolio manager or an investment committee. A fast answer without a source gets ignored. A slightly slower answer with a page reference gets used.

  3. Compliance Architecture Decides Whether Pilots Scale

    Funds cannot move beyond isolated pilots until they have a way to govern permissions and keep sensitive data inside their own environment. Several firms had pilots that worked technically and then waited months for answers on information barriers and data residency.

  4. The Challenges Are the Same Everywhere

    Fund size, strategy and location varied a lot around the tables, yet the hurdles were identical. Small long/short funds and large multi-manager firms were asking the same questions about permissions, audit trails and context.

  5. A Fund's Corrections Are Its Intellectual Property

    Every time an analyst corrects an AI output, that correction captures the fund's judgment. The room's view was that contracts should state clearly who owns those corrections and what a provider is allowed to keep.

Our Take

These are the problems we have worked on since our first institutional deployments: a context graph the fund owns, answers with citations back to the source page, and deployment inside the fund's own cloud or on premises with its permissions carried through. We continued the conversation with Atlas Technica in July at a breakfast in Washington, DC.

Thank you to Alex and the Atlas Technica team for co-hosting, and to everyone who joined us.
