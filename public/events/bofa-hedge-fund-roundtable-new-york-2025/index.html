From Copilots to Agents: A New York Roundtable With Bank of America Prime Brokerage Consulting

In December 2025, we joined Bank of America's Prime Brokerage Consulting team in New York for a closed-door roundtable with more than 20 hedge funds on how they are putting AI agents to work.

Date
December 2025
Venue
New York
Format
Closed-door roundtable
Host
Bank of America Prime Brokerage Consulting

About the Event

New York's largest hedge funds tend to adopt new technology quietly, and AI is no exception. Bank of America's Prime Brokerage Consulting team invited us to a private roundtable with research, technology and operations leaders from more than 20 funds, many of them running AI programs they have not discussed in public.

Vibhav Viswanathan and Mithun Madhusudan, our co-founders, spent the week in New York around the session. The clearest message was that these funds have moved on from AI tools that summarize documents and are now deploying agents that complete whole workflows.

What the Funds Told Us

  1. Summaries No Longer Give Funds an Edge

    Every fund at the table already had tools that summarize filings and transcripts, and none saw them as an advantage. The discussion went straight to agents that finish a piece of work: pulling the filings, updating the model, flagging what changed and drafting a note for review.

  2. Early Deployments Are Showing Measurable Results

    Funds with agents in production described gains in three areas: about 40% of research time freed up, coverage universes 1.5 to 2 times larger with the same team, and decisions after a market event reached twice as fast.

  3. Clear Limits Are What Let Compliance Say Yes

    The firms furthest along set explicit rules for what an agent is never allowed to do, such as touching certain data or sending anything outside the firm. These limits, often called negative constraints, give compliance teams the confidence to approve wider use.

  4. Autonomy Grows in Stages

    Funds described a clear progression: assistants that answer questions, then agents that complete a task under review, then agents that run a full workflow on a schedule. We have since written up these stages in How Investment Firms Adopt AI.

  5. Sensitive Work Stays Inside the Firm

    Data privacy shaped every architecture decision. Funds want sensitive reasoning to run locally or in their own cloud, and keep public AI services for tasks that involve no proprietary information.

Our Take

This shift is what we build for: autonomous agents that run inside a firm's own environment, within limits the firm sets, with every step recorded. Our deployment at a top-20 US hedge fund shows what this looks like in practice.

Thank you to Bank of America's Prime Brokerage Consulting team for hosting, and to every fund that joined the conversation.
