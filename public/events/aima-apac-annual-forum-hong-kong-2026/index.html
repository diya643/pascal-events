AIMA APAC Annual Forum 2026: AI as a Tool, a Theme and a Threat

We closed our October week in Hong Kong at the AIMA APAC Annual Forum, where hedge fund managers, allocators and regulators from across Asia Pacific discussed how AI is changing the industry.

Date
October 29, 2026
Venue

Format

About the Event

The Alternative Investment Management Association's annual Asia Pacific forum is one of the region's largest gatherings of alternative investment managers, investors and regulators. It came at the end of a busy week for us: two days earlier we co-hosted a roundtable dinner with Bare Cove Technology, and the day before we spoke at the ECI AI Forum.

We went to hear how managers across the region are using AI and what is holding them back. AI came up in three ways during the day: as a tool inside firms, as an investment theme and as a security risk. AIMA's survey of more than 100 fund managers, published two weeks later, backed up much of what we heard.

Six Takeaways

  1. Almost Every Manager Now Uses AI

    Nearly every manager is now implementing generative AI, with 97% saying so in AIMA's survey. Investment research is where firms see the most value, followed by operations, technology and compliance. The question has moved from whether to use AI to how to use it well.

  2. Judgment Stays With People

    The discussion kept returning to where AI should stop and people should decide. Managers expect AI to change analyst work significantly, yet none in AIMA's survey saw portfolio manager roles as at risk. Accountability for investment decisions stays with people.

  3. Accuracy and Data Leakage Hold Firms Back

    The biggest barriers to wider use are accuracy, named by 58% of managers, and the risk of data leaking, named by 51%. Only 8% named cost. Both barriers come down to how a system is built: answers that cite their sources, and data that never leaves the firm.

  4. Firms Allow Several AI Models at Once

    Most managers permit more than one public AI model, and the mix keeps changing. That makes it important to send each task to the model best suited to it, and to switch models without rebuilding workflows.

  5. Investors Want Evidence Over Reputation

    Allocators described choosing managers on data, scalability and track record more than on reputation. They also use AI in their own due diligence, and some worry it could make their decisions more alike.

  6. Outsourcing Technology Keeps the Responsibility In-House

    AI has made cyberattacks cheaper to launch, and managers were reminded that relying on outside providers leaves the responsibility with the firm. The same applies to AI providers, and over a third of managers expect to rely less on external service providers.

Our Take

The week in Hong Kong gave us one consistent message: managers want AI they can trust with their own data, under their own controls. That is what we build for hedge funds and asset managers across the region, from the context graph to decision traces.
