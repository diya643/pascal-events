AI Workshop 2026 With Goldman Sachs Prime Services Consulting: What We Learned in Singapore and Hong Kong

In May, we joined Goldman Sachs Prime Services Consulting's AI Workshop 2026 for hedge fund managers in Singapore and Hong Kong, and spent the rest of the week meeting funds in both cities.

Dates
May 19 (Singapore) and May 20 (Hong Kong), 2026
Audience
Hedge fund managers
Format
Invite-only workshop
Host
Goldman Sachs Prime Services Consulting

About the Event

Goldman Sachs Prime Services Consulting brought hedge fund managers together on May 19 in Singapore and May 20 in Hong Kong for a day on AI. The program opened with a panel on AI governance and then moved into practical workshops, each led by an AI provider and focused on one area of a fund's work. We were invited to lead one of them.

Vibhav Viswanathan and Mithun Madhusudan, our co-founders, also used the trip to meet hedge funds privately in both cities. We wanted to understand how far Asian funds have gone with AI and what is holding them back.

What We Learned

  1. Asian Funds Are as Advanced as Their Western Peers

    The most sophisticated funds in Hong Kong and Singapore are using AI as deeply as the leading funds we work with in New York and London. Many of them are also the most private about it, which makes their progress easy to underestimate.

  2. Agents Are Moving Into the Investment Process

    The leading funds have moved past chat assistants. They are building AI agents that run parts of the investment process, such as earnings preparation, screening, model updates and monitoring, with a person approving each result.

  3. Good AI Depends on a Clean Data Foundation

    Almost every conversation came back to data. Funds are reviewing their data infrastructure, market data and alternative data, because an agent can only be as good as the information it can reach and trust.

  4. Governance Comes Before the Demo

    Starting the day with governance set the tone. Managers wanted to understand how a provider handles permissions, data retention and model choice before they wanted to see a product, and the questions in our session followed the same order.

  5. Managers Compare Providers on Practical Questions

    Seeing several providers in one day made comparison easy. The questions that mattered were practical: where the system runs, whether it works with the fund's own data and templates, and whether every answer can be traced to a source.

Our Take

The week confirmed what we are building toward: infrastructure that lets funds run AI agents on their own context, through the Pascal platform and Pascal Agents, with permissions and an audit trail at every step. We describe the stages funds move through in How Investment Firms Adopt AI.

Thank you to Goldman Sachs Prime Services Consulting for including us, and to every manager who joined our session.
