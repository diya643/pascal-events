Global Alts Miami 2026: Six Signals on AI in Alternative Investments

We attended Global Alts Miami 2026 to meet hedge funds, family offices and allocators, and to understand how AI is changing the way managers raise capital and how investors assess them.

Dates
February 23 to 26, 2026
Venue
Miami Beach Convention Center
Format
Conference
Organizer
iConnections, co-hosted by MFA

About the Event

Global Alts Miami is one of the largest meeting points between alternative investment managers and the investors who allocate to them. Because the event is built around raising capital, it is a good place to hear AI discussed from both sides: what investors now expect from managers, and what managers can show them.

Our team spent the week in meetings with hedge funds, family offices and fund administrators, and attended the sessions closest to our work. These are the signals we brought back.

Six Signals

  1. Investors Are Using AI to Assess Managers

    Allocators now use AI to review managers in more depth, and much of the operational due diligence process can be supported by it. That raises the bar for managers: their documents, data and processes need to be organized well enough to hold up to closer scrutiny.

  2. Reading Is No Longer the Bottleneck

    When every investor can read and summarize documents almost instantly, reading capacity stops being an advantage. The edge moves to what a firm knows that others do not: its research history, its models and the reasoning behind its past decisions.

  3. The Most Valuable Data Is Inside the Firm

    Managers were less interested in buying more market data than in making their own memos, models and meeting notes usable. The information that sets a firm apart already sits inside it.

  4. Large Funds Are Setting the Pace

    Multi-strategy funds continue to lead on performance and on technology spending. Their scale lets them build AI teams in-house, and smaller managers asked us how to reach a similar level of capability without the same headcount.

  5. Investors Can Spot AI Washing

    Vague claims about AI now count against a manager. Investors want to see where AI sits in the process, what it is allowed to access and who reviews its output.

  6. Private Credit Needs Consistent Data

    Private credit managers often define the same terms in different ways, which makes comparing deals and reporting to investors harder than it should be. This is where AI that extracts and classifies data from documents helps most, and private credit managers were among the most engaged people we met.

Our Take

The common thread in Miami: as AI makes public information cheap to process, a manager's advantage depends on its own context and on proving its process to investors. We build that layer for hedge funds and private equity and credit firms, with every answer cited back to its source.
